Signal is putting a barrier between private conversations and AI assistants that can read phone screens. Its November 20 announcement describes new screenshot restrictions on iOS 27 and automatic activation of Android’s Screen security when Signal detects an assistant accessing the screen. The practical question extends beyond one messaging app: **which parts of your device should an assistant be allowed to see?** [Read Signal’s announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/).

## Encryption and screen access are different boundaries

An assistant does not have to break encryption to read a message displayed on a device. Android Authority’s reporting explains the tradeoff in Signal’s response: the protections also prevent ordinary screenshots, and users can disable them. Signal says its detection does not cover every Android version, its approach does not provide equivalent screenshot protection on macOS, and screen blocking cannot address other access paths such as local files. That makes this a useful defense with limits, not a complete privacy guarantee. [Read the independent report](https://www.androidauthority.com/signal-blocks-gemini-siri-ai-private-messages-3737647/).

There are legitimate reasons to let an assistant understand a screen. Apple describes Siri’s onscreen awareness as a way to ask questions and take actions about what you are viewing. Google’s Gemini documentation explains how screen-action chips upload context, and how Intelligent Screen Context can use it when you ask about your screen. Those capabilities do not establish that every conversation is continuously uploaded. They do make the settings governing access important. [Apple’s feature explanation](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/), [Google’s screen-context documentation](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15850607?hl=en).

## Three places to check

- **Signal:** review its screen-protection setting before switching it off to take a screenshot. The convenience change also affects protection against AI capture.
- **Gemini on Android:** Google documents controls under Settings → Screen context for using screen text and screenshots. Check the options available on your device; menus can differ across versions.
- **Siri on iOS 27.2:** EFF documents Settings → Siri → Excluded Apps, where you can add apps whose contents you do not want Siri AI to access. Exclusion also removes related assistant features. [EFF’s guide](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/victory-you-can-now-exclude-apps-siri-ai).

These are checks against published documentation, not results from our own handset testing. A recipient’s device and permissions also matter; your settings cannot control every copy of a conversation.

**Our view:** useful AI should come with understandable boundaries. People should be able to exclude sensitive apps without navigating a maze of settings or losing unrelated functions. Signal’s workaround shows both the value of a protective default and the need for operating systems to provide better controls.
