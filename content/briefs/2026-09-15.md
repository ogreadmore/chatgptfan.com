Today's brief pairs a service update with a practical reading note. OpenAI's Work Mode incident is marked resolved; the background resources below address how to plan for AI systems that sometimes fail.

## Work Mode recovery

[OpenAI's incident notice](https://status.openai.com/incidents/01M2GA8XTS6VB3QCDEGZ0HNAQ5), checked this morning, says the affected services have recovered. Earlier updates described some ChatGPT Plus users encountering errors when starting or resuming Work Mode tasks, or having limited access to workspace tools and files. The page records a resolution update on September 15.

This is OpenAI's service account. The notice also cautions that aggregate availability does not describe every customer's experience. A resolved incident is useful information if you were waiting to retry a task; it does not establish that a particular task completed successfully.

Our practical suggestion: before repeating an interrupted action, check what actually finished. For work involving files or another service, inspect the resulting document or destination first. That can help avoid duplicate work or accidentally repeating an action whose outcome was unclear.

## A framework for the less visible failures

An outage is an obvious failure. An answer that looks plausible but does not meet the task's requirements can be harder to notice. Our view is that a useful AI workflow should plan for both.

[NIST's AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) offers voluntary guidance for considering risks across the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI systems. Its overview also links to a companion Playbook and a profile addressing risks specific to generative AI. The framework was released in January 2023; this is background reading, not a new announcement. NIST currently notes that version 1.0 is being revised.

For a small project, our suggested starting point is a short record of what the AI can change, how you will check the result, and what happens if the service becomes unavailable. Keep a copy of essential inputs and decide which actions need a person to review them before they take effect. These are our workflow suggestions, not a claim that completing a checklist proves a system safe.

**Our view:** recovery notices help with today's interruption. Clear responsibility and a workable fallback help with the next one. Neither replaces scrutiny of the broader risks posed by increasingly capable AI.
