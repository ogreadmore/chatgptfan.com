Washington’s next AI export push may come with a list of favored companies. Reuters reported late Sunday that the Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar with the plans, said the Trump administration intends to designate more than two dozen AI “national champions.” The reported names include Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic. The aim is to help them expand overseas and compete with Chinese rivals. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report; this is not a confirmed final roster. [Reuters report](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/trump-administration-name-nvidia-other-041705165.html)

There is already a concrete policy behind the export push. A July 2025 executive order established the American AI Exports Program, asking for proposals that combine computing hardware, cloud infrastructure, data pipelines, models and applications. The order describes support for selected packages, potentially including loans, guarantees, insurance and other assistance. Availability depends on applicable law and funding. [Executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/promoting-the-export-of-the-american-ai-technology-stack/)

That helps explain why the story matters beyond a company receiving a flattering label. An overseas customer could be choosing a collection of infrastructure and services that work together, with government support helping the suppliers compete. The order asks proposals to identify target markets and the entities that would build, own and operate the infrastructure. It also requires security measures and compliance with relevant export controls. [Program requirements](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/promoting-the-export-of-the-american-ai-technology-stack/)

One naming wrinkle is worth keeping straight. In February, the White House described a “National Champions Initiative” that would bring **partner countries’ leading companies** into the American AI technology stack. That earlier description is distinct from Sunday’s reported designation of US suppliers. It does not confirm the new list. [February announcement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/02/u-s-promotes-ai-adoption-sovereignty-and-exports-at-india-ai-impact-summit/)

For the next announcement, look past the roster: which projects receive support, on what terms, and what can the public inspect? For anyone buying these services, the practical questions remain who operates them, what happens to your data, and how you leave if the arrangement stops working.

Our view: export promotion and independent safety scrutiny serve different purposes. A government’s decision to help a supplier win business should not substitute for evidence about a particular system’s capabilities, limitations and safeguards. We should be able to examine both the commercial terms and that evidence.
