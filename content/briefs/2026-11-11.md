A client who asks for an AI-generated video may also need someone to edit the sequence, add sound and deliver a cut worth watching. That distinction helps explain an apparent puzzle in a new study from OpenAI and Upwork: freelance projects that called for AI advertised much bigger budgets, but the advertised price of an hour barely changed.

The researchers compared postings within the same kind of work and the same month. For fixed-price jobs, the stated budget was about **52% higher** when the job involved AI. For hourly postings, the advertised rate was only **1.5% higher**. These are offers from clients. Neither figure tells you what a freelancer took home. [Read the full study, especially Exhibit 2.4](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/openai-upwork-full-report.pdf)

## Where the extra money might go

The authors could see hours on a narrower set of jobs: hourly contracts that resulted in a hire and recorded some work. In the first 90 days, the AI-related contracts logged about **41% more hours** after the same adjustments. Those were different contracts from the fixed-price jobs. Still, the extra hours fit something the researchers saw in the descriptions: clients were asking for combinations of skills they rarely used to request together, including video production and editing AI-generated footage. [OpenAI’s overview](https://openai.com/index/ai-effect-on-freelance-work/)

A bigger brief for one editor can coexist with fewer openings for another worker. In an earlier, independent study, researchers found that postings for writing and coding work susceptible to automation fell 21% **relative to work requiring more manual skills** in the eight months after ChatGPT launched. That was a different period and a different comparison. Neither study tells us whether freelancers as a whole are better off. [Read the peer-reviewed study in Management Science](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420)

If you’re quoting a video job, ask the client what counts as finished: three generated shots, or an edited film with sound, captions and two rounds of revisions? Price the work in that answer. A larger number at the top of a posting is little comfort if it buys twice as much of your week.
