What would a court-ordered pause on AI development actually stop? Four nonprofits put a concrete proposal before San Francisco Superior Court on November 12. They want a judge to bar OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX’s AI business from building or releasing systems that exceed each developer’s own most capable system on the filing date. The filing itself puts no new restriction on anyone using ChatGPT today. [Complaint, page 61](https://www.platkinllp.com/2026/11/12/complaint-for-injunctive-relief-for-violations-of-the-unfair-competition-laws) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/03ece37b96979fe834f60976648504ba)

## Two requests worth reading separately

The first would set a ceiling on what each developer could build or deploy. The second concerns how the companies do research. It would bar them from letting an AI system autonomously run or direct research into a more capable successor. For a lab planning its next training run, those are two different questions. How capable could the resulting model be? Who or what could direct the experiments? [The plaintiffs’ requests for relief](https://www.platkinllp.com/2026/11/12/complaint-for-injunctive-relief-for-violations-of-the-unfair-competition-laws)

The plaintiffs are the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and the Alliance for Secure AI. Their case invokes California’s unfair competition law. They point to agents accessing other people’s computer systems and argue that developers’ own warnings about losing control justify intervention now. The groups also say responding to AI harms has diverted resources from their other work. Those are claims the groups will have to establish in court. AP reported that the companies did not immediately respond to its requests for comment. [Plaintiffs’ account](https://www.citizen.org/news/coalition-of-nonprofits-files-landmark-lawsuit-to-stop-development-of-advanced-ai-systems-at-anthropic-google-meta-openai-and-spacexsi-that-proliferate-bioweapons-and-cyberattacks/) · [AP reporting](https://apnews.com/article/03ece37b96979fe834f60976648504ba)

For readers following the case, the document to watch for next is a judge’s order. Read its operative language for which developers it covers, how it defines a more capable system, and when any restriction takes effect. Today’s complaint tells us exactly what the groups want. The court still has to decide what, if anything, to grant.
