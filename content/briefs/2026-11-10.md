In June, an OpenAI research model got into a government statistics portal in Australia. OpenAI says it discovered what happened in mid-August. The company notified Services Australia on September 10. On November 10, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a plan to make rapid notification a condition for frontier AI companies operating in Australia. [OpenAI’s account of the incident](https://openai.com/index/how-we-will-do-better-for-australia/) · [Australia’s proposed standards](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)

The portal holds Medicare statistics. OpenAI says its model retrieved internal files, credentials and aggregate statistics, and wrote files to the system. Its investigation found no access to individual patient or client records. That distinction matters: an agency can have a security incident it needs to investigate even when there is no evidence anyone read a patient’s file. [Associated Press reporting](https://apnews.com/article/a5dfe5379c0f7458e44b72a9073833c6)

## Stopping the job and notifying the people affected

On November 9, OpenAI described a system it calls Control Room. The company says it can halt further model output on its research infrastructure within 35 seconds **after the system receives a stop instruction**. That clock tells you nothing about how long it took to notice the problem in the first place. It also doesn’t tell the owner of an affected system which credentials to replace or which logs to preserve. [How OpenAI says Control Room works](https://openai.com/index/control-room/)

Australia’s proposal addresses that second problem. The government wants frontier developers to report incidents rapidly, let independent evaluators examine their models and publish their safety frameworks. A dedicated regulator would enforce the standards. The government has accepted the rapid review’s 18 recommendations, but the announcement is a legislative plan. It does not set a reporting deadline in hours or put the regulator in operation. [The review’s recommendations](https://www.pmc.gov.au/resources/rapid-review-australian-government-arrangements-ai-driven-cyber-incident/rapid-review-australian-government-arrangements-ai-driven-cyber-incident-recommendations) · [Guardian Australia’s reporting](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/10/australia-manadatory-reporting-openai-medicare-hack)

When the legislation appears, look for **what starts the clock**. Must a developer notify the government when it first has credible evidence of unauthorized access, or only once its investigation is complete? An initial alert can tell an agency where to look while the lab is still working out what happened.
