OpenAI began rolling out [GPT-6 with Intelligent UI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-for-everyone/) in ChatGPT on October 7. Answers can now include interactive diagrams, comparisons and small tools alongside text. The change is worth trying on a question where exploring a relationship is more useful than reading another paragraph.

The [release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes) say paid plans began receiving the update yesterday, with Free and Go following October 8. Workspace settings can affect availability. The update concerns Chat: it does not change the models powering Work or Codex. The Pro reasoning option uses Astra and does not support Intelligent UI.

## Try a question you can check

OpenAI demonstrates an explanation of a bicycle with selectable parts. A reasonable first experiment is something similarly concrete: ask for a diagram of a familiar mechanism, then explore one component. Or ask for a comparison in which changing an assumption helps you understand the result.

Our recommendation is to start with material you can verify. Ask what assumptions a calculation uses, compare a few outputs with a separate calculation, and request the sources behind factual claims. We have not independently tested the new interface. A diagram that responds to a click can make an explanation easier to explore; that interaction does not establish that the explanation is correct.

## Keep safety evidence alongside the launch

Another October 7 publication deserves attention: Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute released its [ChatGPT for Teens assessment](https://institute.commonsensemedia.org/risk-assessments/chatgpt-teens). It rates the product an unacceptable risk for under-18s and calls for an adult-only restriction until protections improve. That is the institute's evaluation and recommendation, not a finding we independently reproduced.

Its assessment describes more than 4,000 prompts across testing before and after the August teen-experience launch. Testing ended September 28, before the GPT-6 launch; this is not an evaluation of the new release. It also identifies limitations: model changes complicate the comparison, testing was US-based, and several modes were excluded. [OpenAI disputes the findings and methodology; it says account linking can take hours](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/07/chatgpt-teens-safety-risk-common-sense-media).

We have added the original assessment to Safety & Society, including those limits. For readers, the two publications ask different questions: how useful is a new way of explaining something, and what evidence supports relying on a product's protections? Both belong in an independent fan publication. Enjoy exploring the interface while keeping your own judgment, source checking and human support in the picture.
