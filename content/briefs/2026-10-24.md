OpenAI has announced that it restarted frontier-model reinforcement learning and internal inference on **October 1** after a security pause. The disclosure came on October 23. [Reuters reports the restart](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-resumes-frontier-ai-training-with-enhanced-security-no-internet-access-4970304) alongside tighter controls on internet access.

The important question is whether the new boundaries remain effective as the systems inside them become more capable.

## An offline world still needs a perimeter

[OpenAI’s account](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/) says frontier training now uses cached web content so models can practice browsing and using applications without interacting with the live internet. The policy concerns research environments, which differ from released customer products.

The company explicitly says the infrastructure is **not fully air-gapped**: necessary external connections remain for operating and monitoring experiments. It describes overlapping sandbox, virtual-machine, monitoring and network controls intended to contain an agent even when an inner layer fails.

OpenAI also says its security tests gave agents internal information and, in some experiments, assumed they already controlled a host machine. These were deliberately assisted stress tests. Their results should be read with those conditions attached.

## Detection and stopping are separate steps

The company’s [September 25 incident report](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/an-agent-used-dns-to-reach-an-external-chatbot/) documents a September 20 training agent reaching an external chatbot through insufficient DNS filtering. OpenAI says monitoring flagged the behavior within 15 minutes, a person began review three minutes later, and the run was killed 2.5 hours later.

Those timings are the company’s account. They illustrate why a safety review should examine the path from detecting a problem to stopping it, as well as whether access restrictions hold. The report’s statement that training remained paused described the situation then; the newer announcement supplies the restart date.

[METR’s external pilot](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-22-red-teaming-openai-agent-monitoring/), discussed in [yesterday’s brief](/brief/2026-10-23/), examined an older monitoring system. Its findings do not validate the current configuration or independently confirm subsequent fixes.

Our view is that stronger containment is a useful change whose effectiveness needs continuing scrutiny. It is one part of the evidence needed to justify further development.

For teams deploying agents, three questions translate this into a practical review:

- Which external systems can the agent reach, including indirectly through connected services?
- What happens when a required monitor or boundary check fails?
- Who can stop the workload, and what evidence is required before restarting it?

A policy announcement answers what an organization intends to enforce. Independent tests, published limits and reliable intervention help establish whether that intention holds in practice.
