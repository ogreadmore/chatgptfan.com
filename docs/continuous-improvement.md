# Continuous editorial improvement

The owner expects the publication to improve as the field changes. Maintenance must protect quality and improve reader value, not merely refresh dates. Keep the approved design and editorial independence.

## Every daily run

- Ask what a reader can understand or do better after this edition. Prefer a clear answer over a shallow list of announcements. Compare candidate leads by impact, evidence, relevance and what is new since prior coverage; look beyond the feeds.
- Inspect recent editions for repeated themes and missed consequential stories. A new development can justify returning to a topic, but state what changed. Keep practical use, learning, useful projects and economic evidence visible alongside safety and policy. No artificial subject quota.
- Assess social and video selections for actual substance and freshness. Clearly label older material, archive access and company claims. Retire low-value duplicates; do not substitute feed success for editorial judgment.
- Inspect every section for reader friction, dead ends, dated recommendations, unclear costs, weak guidance, visual defects or inaccessible controls. Repair routine issues and verify the result. Do not redesign, add promotions or change the campaign’s demand autonomously.
- Artwork must communicate a coherent story from its pixels, with the meaning-first and two-pass review. A passing checklist must describe actual observation. Any uncertainty or owner rejection overrides prior approval.
- Record one useful improvement candidate, a completed correction, or a reason no change is warranted in the private dated log. Keep unresolved candidates in docs/daily-runs/improvement-backlog.md, with evidence, reader benefit, next action and review date. Avoid vague recurring “review later” items.

## Monday quality review

Review the last seven editions for evidence quality, independent sourcing, important omissions, repetitive angles, clear takeaways and corrections. Check whether featured resources and social/video selections still earn their space. Review feed failures, source-review backlog, dependency advisories and accessibility/navigation behavior. Prioritize a small number of changes by reader benefit and effort; close resolved items. Use available aggregate analytics only when access is authorized and working; never fabricate performance or change collection settings merely to get measurements.

## Monthly

Follow the full library audit in resource-maintenance.md. Reassess the source mix, emerging tools, reader paths, maintenance burden, performance and accessibility. Suggest larger opportunities with concrete evidence and a reviewable proposal. More links, more generated art and more automation are not quality measures on their own.

## Evidence standard

Record exactly which sources and rendered states were checked. Keep “blocked,” “partially reviewed” and “reviewed with no change” distinct. Do not advance review dates on redirects, snippets or status codes alone. Automated tests protect structural rules; they do not validate an article’s truth or an illustration’s meaning.
